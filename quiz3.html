UNIT 4.7.0 CONCEPTUAL FOUNDATIONS

Foundations of Adjusting Entries: Periodicity & The Accrual Basis

In short
TLDR — Unit 4.7.0: A business operates continuously, but accountants must slice time into artificial reporting periods (months, quarters, years) under the periodicity assumption. Because cash flow does not coincide with economic consumption or delivery, adjusting entries are required at the end of each period under the accrual basis (PAS 1) to ensure revenues are recorded when earned and expenses when incurred. Every adjusting entry touches at least one Statement of Financial Position account and one Statement of Profit or Loss account—and never touches Cash.

4.7.0.1 Continuous Business vs. Artificial Reporting Periods

💡 The Core Principle of Adjusting Entries

A commercial business operates continuously without stopping, but owners, banks, and the BIR need scorecards for fixed periods: a month, a quarter, a year. When we draw an artificial cut-off line at the period-end, economic transactions are caught midway in motion. Adjusting entries are internal journal entries made at cut-off to assign each peso of revenue and expense to the period it truly belongs to.

Under the going concern assumption (Unit 2), we expect an enterprise to operate indefinitely. If accountants waited until a company finally closed its doors before calculating profit, management and investors would wait decades for financial feedback. To make timely decisions, the time period assumption (periodicity) divides business life into equal, artificial reporting slices:

  • Monthly periods: For internal store managers checking monthly operational targets.
  • Quarterly periods (3 months): For commercial bank loan monitoring and listed corporate reports.
  • Annual periods (12 months): The primary fiscal or calendar year ending December 31 for audited financial statements and BIR tax returns.

At 11:58 PM on Thursday night, December 31, 2026, driver Kuya Jun is steering the Animo delivery truck down Roxas Boulevard, rushing express parcels to customers. The year ends, but the business doesn't.

4.7.0.2 Cash Basis vs. Accrual Basis of Accounting

To understand why adjusting entries exist, we must contrast the two operational bases of accounting. Consider everyday student life:

  • The LRT Beep Card: When you load ₱500 at the station teller, you haven't taken a ride yet. Under the cash basis, the entire ₱500 is treated as an "expense" the moment you pay. Under the accrual basis, loading the card creates an asset (Prepaid Fare); the expense occurs each time you tap the turnstile to ride.
  • Ate Gina's Carinderia Tab (Pakilista): Regular patrons eat lunch on credit and settle at month-end. Under the cash basis, Ate Gina would record zero sales while feeding 100 students daily, followed by an artificial spike on payday. Under the accrual basis, she records revenue the moment meals are served.
Feature / Dimension Cash Basis Accounting Accrual Basis Accounting (PAS 1 / PFRS)
Revenue Recognition Recorded only when cash is received in hand or bank. Recorded when earned (performance delivered), regardless of cash timing.
Expense Recognition Recorded only when cash is physically disbursed. Recorded when incurred (goods/services consumed), regardless of cash timing.
Matching Principle Ignored. Profit fluctuates based on payment timing. Applied. Expenses are matched with related revenues in the period.
PFRS Standing Not compliant with PFRS for general-purpose financial reporting. Required under PFRS (PAS 1); standard in FDNACCT.
Adjusting Entries None required (only cash moves are booked). Needed at every period-end.

4.7.0.3 The Golden Rules of Adjusting Entries

Every adjusting entry follows two rules:

Rule 1: One balance-sheet account, one income-statement account

Every adjusting entry changes one account on the balance sheet and one account on the income statement at the same time.

Rule 2: Cash is NEVER Involved

Cash is never debited, and Cash is never credited, in an adjusting journal entry. Adjustments reflect the internal passage of time. If money is physically moving on December 31, it is an ordinary operational transaction.

  • Timing: Economic service used NOW; cash paid in the FUTURE.

4.7.1.6 Common Traps & Exam Tips

Trap 1: Crediting Cash

Never credit Cash in Bank in an adjusting entry. Cash has not moved at cut-off. The credit is always an accrued liability account (2xx).

Trap 2: Shortcut Day Counting

Do not assume 3 calendar months equals 90 days. A note issued October 1 adjusting to December 31 has 91 elapsed days (30 in Oct + 30 in Nov + 31 in Dec).

Trap 3: 360 vs 365 Days

Under ordinary interest (Banker's rule, Unit 4.5), time in days is divided by 360, not 365, unless exact interest is explicitly specified.

Trap 4: Philippine Holidays

In introductory FDNACCT conventions, treat all weekdays (including Rizal Day, New Year's Eve, and New Year's Day) as regular working days.

Quiz 3 • Unit 4.7.5
Executive Summary: Depreciation Core Logic
Under PAS 16, depreciation is a process of cost allocation, not asset valuation. The annual formula is (Cost − Residual Value) ÷ Useful Life. Because wear-and-tear does not diminish legal ownership, we never credit Equipment directly; we credit Accumulated Depreciation (151), a contra-asset account. Depreciation Expense (610) is a temporary account that closes to zero annually, whereas Accumulated Depreciation builds up year by year on the balance sheet. Land is never depreciated.

4.7.5.1 Economic Concept: Cost Allocation, Not Valuation

📌 Key Takeaway (Section 4.7.5.1)

Depreciation does not attempt to measure what you could sell the asset for on second-hand platforms today. Instead, it measures how much of the original cost was consumed to generate revenue during the current fiscal period.

🔢 Computational Blueprint: Calculating Depreciation Outside Journal Entries
1. Depreciable Base: Historical Cost − Residual Value (Salvage Value)
Example: ₱900,000 cost − ₱100,000 salvage = ₱800,000.00.
2. Annual Straight-Line Depreciation: Depreciable Base ÷ Useful Life in Years
₱800,000 ÷ 5 years = ₱160,000.00 / full year.
3. Partial-Year Proration: Annual Depreciation × (Months in Service ÷ 12)
Acquired April 1 (9 months in 2026): ₱160,000 × (9 ÷ 12) = ₱120,000.00 (Depreciation Expense).
4. Carrying Amount (Book Value): Historical Cost − Accumulated Depreciation
₱900,000 − ₱120,000 = ₱780,000.00.

Unit 4.7.6: Doubtful Accounts — The Allowance Method & Net Realizable Value

Valuation of trade receivables under the accrual basis, contra-asset architecture, aging schedules, and corporate reporting implications.

Quiz 3 • Unit 4.7.6
Executive Summary: Valuation of Accounts Receivable
Under PFRS and the matching principle, businesses granting credit must anticipate uncollectible accounts and record Doubtful Accounts Expense in the same accounting period that credit sales occur. Because specific defaulted customers cannot be identified at period-end, we do not credit Accounts Receivable directly. Instead, we credit a contra-asset account: Allowance for Doubtful Accounts (Account 109). This reduces gross receivables to their Net Realizable Value (NRV) on the Statement of Financial Position.
The Matching Principle

Expense Matches Revenue

Bad debt losses are an inevitable cost of extending credit. Recognizing doubtful accounts in the period of sale ensures current-period operating expenses are properly matched against current credit sales.

Contra-Asset Architecture

Account 109 (Credit Normal)

Allowance for Doubtful Accounts has a normal credit balance. It acts as an offset against gross Accounts Receivable (Account 102), preserving the legal gross claims while reporting estimated net cash flows.

Valuation Standard

Net Realizable Value (NRV)

Receivables must be presented at the net amount expected to be collected in cash: NRV = Gross Accounts Receivable − Allowance for Doubtful Accounts. Omission overstates current assets and equity.

💡 Economic Logic of Landed Cost: Why Add Freight-In & Deduct Purchase Discounts?

Under PAS 2 (Inventories), inventory is recorded at total acquisition cost—the net economic sacrifice necessary to bring items to their present location and condition for sale.

1. WHY ADD FREIGHT-IN? (Landed Transport Cost)

Goods in a distant warehouse cannot be sold on Taft Avenue. Paying ₱4,000 courier freight brings the goods onto store shelves. If 1,000 units invoice at ₱150 each (₱150,000), total sacrifice is ₱154,000 (₱154/unit). Ignoring freight and pricing at ₱152 causes a ₱2 loss per unit! Freight-In must be capitalized into inventory cost.

2. WHY DEDUCT PURCHASE DISCOUNTS? (Cash Savings)

Taking a 2% prompt-payment discount on a ₱150,000 bill saves ₱3,000 in cash. The entity only parted with ₱147,000 cash. Under historical cost, assets must reflect true net sacrifice. Recording ₱150,000 when only paying ₱147,000 artificially inflates asset value and understates future profit.

Unified Average Landed Cost Formula:
Unit Landed Cost = (Gross Invoice Purchases + Freight-In − Discounts − Returns) ÷ Net Usable Units Received
Example: (₱150,000 invoice + ₱4,000 freight − ₱2,500 discount − ₱7,500 returns) ÷ 950 units = ₱151.58 / unit.

Crucial Examination Insight: Why ATB Total (₱936,000) ≠ UTB (₱817,500) + AJE (₱182,500)

₱817,500.00 + ₱182,500.00 = ₱1,000,000.00. Why is the ATB total ₱936,000 instead? Because when an adjustment reduces an existing balance (e.g., crediting Prepaid Supplies ₱14k, Prepaid Insurance ₱8k, Prepaid Rent ₱27k, or debiting Unearned Revenue ₱15k), those adjustments subtract rather than add to account balances. Total reductions = ₱14,000 + ₱8,000 + ₱27,000 + ₱15,000 = ₱64,000.00. Exactly ₱1,000,000.00 − ₱64,000.00 = ₱936,000.00!

Official Formula Guide Key Reminders

Mandatory Departmental Rules for Quiz 3
  • Prepaid Expenses: Use the ASSET METHOD ONLY. The adjusting entry always records the expired/used portion (Dr Expense / Cr Prepaid Asset).
  • Unearned Income: Use the LIABILITY METHOD ONLY. The adjusting entry always records the earned portion (Dr Unearned Liability / Cr Revenue).
  • Depreciation: Straight-line only. Depreciable Cost = Cost − Residual Value. If acquired on days 1–15, include the month; if acquired on days 16+, start depreciation the next month. Net Carrying Amount (NCA) = Cost − Accumulated Depreciation.
  • Doubtful Accounts: Allowance method based on A/R. Target ADA end = A/R × rate (or aging sum). Doubtful Accounts Expense = Required Ending ADA − Unadjusted Beginning ADA (credit). If beginning ADA is a debit, add it. NRV = A/R − Required Ending ADA.
  • Ending Inventory: Periodic system adjustment is Dr Merchandise Inventory / Cr Income Summary for the exact physical count amount.
  • Interest Accrual: Use 360 days for ordinary interest when term is in days; use 12 months when term is in months. Month-end cut-off accrues only elapsed days/months.
  • Every Adjustment Shows Three Things: (1) The adjustment amount, (2) The expense used / revenue earned for the period, and (3) The period-end balance sheet balance.
